### Misconception check
- **"The cap hole is to stop the ink drying out or to equalise pressure."** This is mostly wrong. BIC gives safety and ISO 11540 as the reason. BIC does mention a secondary benefit: vented caps "allow more air to circulate around the pen point". That benefit is real, but it isn't why the hole exists.
- **The pressure-equalising hole exists, but it's in the barrel** (F10). That is the twist.
- **"The hole is for an emergency tracheotomy with the pen."** This is a myth. Don't repeat it, even as a joke.

### Claims to avoid
- That the hole "guarantees" survival or "makes it impossible to choke". Say "designed to help keep air flowing until help arrives".
- "Every pen cap has a hole." Adult-only pens and caps too big to swallow are exempt, and some caps vent through grooves instead of a top hole.
- Made-up annual death counts. That includes the viral US "~100 deaths a year", which has no reliable source.
- "No deaths since 1990." It comes from one 2004 report, can't be verified now, and sounds absolute.
- The "3–8%" statistic (F13).
- Any advice to try removing an inhaled cap yourself.

**Safety note for on-screen text and caption:** if someone is choking or struggling to breathe, call emergency services (911 / 999 / 112) immediately.

Design and engineering people share it for the "small design, big reason" angle. The barrel-hole twist drives "I didn't know there were TWO holes" shares.

## 5. Caption, hashtags and alt text

**Caption (SEO-first):**
Why do pen caps have a hole in the top? On many pens, like the BIC Cristal, the hole is a safety vent. It's designed to let air pass if a cap is accidentally inhaled, following the international standard ISO 11540 (at least 8 litres of air per minute). The hole that balances air pressure for the ink is a different one, on the side of the barrel. 🖊️

Safety: if someone is choking or can't breathe, call emergency services (911 / 999 / 112) immediately. Never chew on pen caps.

Sources: BIC FAQ; ISO 11540:2021; BS 7272:1990; Snopes fact-check (2024); Int J Pediatr Otorhinolaryngol (2012).
