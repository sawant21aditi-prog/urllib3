# Reel #3: Why smoke alarms chirp at 3 a.m.

Topic chosen by the Virality Agent (score 23/25). Full research, sources and hook rules are in
`reel_assets/brand/hook_playbook.md`. The fuel-gauge arrow was benched: it can't carry an honest fear hook.

Core claims used on screen (all hedged):
- The chirp is a low-battery warning (First Alert, Kidde).
- Homes are usually coolest around 2–6 a.m.; colder batteries lose voltage, which can trip the low-battery chirp (First Alert, Kidde).
- If it still chirps with a fresh battery, check the manufacture date: replace alarms after about 10 years (NFPA 72 / NFPA).
- Three in five home fire deaths happen in homes with no smoke alarm or no working one (NFPA).

Hook: "That 3 a.m. chirp means your smoke alarm is dying." / "And it's not bad luck it happens at night."
CTA (save): "Save this for the next time it chirps at three a.m." Loops into beat 1.
