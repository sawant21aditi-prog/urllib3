# Experiment: share or save line vs ending on a fact

**Question:** does ending with an explicit call to action raise Shares, and which works better, "Send this to someone who…" or "Save this for…"?

- **Control:** Reel #1 ends on a fact, with no call to action.
- **Test:** Reels #2–#6 end with a spoken and on-screen call to action, alternating send / save / send / save / send. The line must be **specific**: name a person ("someone who chews every pen they touch") or a future moment ("for your next elevator argument"). Never a generic "share this".

**How to log it:** 48 hours after each post, open Instagram Insights for that Reel and fill in its row in `cta_test.csv`:
- views, accounts reached, shares, saves, likes, comments, average watch time
- **Shares per 1k reach** = shares ÷ reach × 1000
- **Saves per 1k reach** = saves ÷ reach × 1000

These two per-reach numbers are the comparison: they control for one Reel simply getting more views.

**Reading it after Reel #6:**
- Compare the average shares per 1k reach of the send Reels against the save Reels and against the control. Do the same for saves per 1k reach.
- With 5 Reels this is a direction, not proof. Topic strength varies a lot, so only act on a clear gap (roughly 1.5× or more).
- Keep the winner as the default ending and re-test every ~10 Reels.
