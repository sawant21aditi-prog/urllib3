# Product lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- The customer moment is easy to describe: a job seeker opens one file and sees a resume they would be proud to send to a specific job. A clear end state like that is the right place to start.
- The scope is already narrow: one deliverable, one revision, a fixed turnaround. Little needs cutting.
- Delivery is all digital and the cost per order is low, so one person can own every touchpoint without handing work to anyone else.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No craft the buyer can't get for free.** The buyer can paste their resume into the same AI tools themselves. If the founder adds no judgment beyond a lightly edited AI draft, the product is generic, and buyers will notice. The founder's resume-writing experience is unconfirmed, so nothing yet backs the "taste" this product depends on.
2. **Seams in the experience.** The order moves from a Fiverr gig or a Reddit/LinkedIn/Discord message to intake, an AI draft, the founder's edit, a Doc/Word file and then a revision. No one has designed that path. Unclear intake (which job? what are the real achievements?) is where tailoring fails, and tailoring is the whole promise.
3. **Unclear focus.** "Clearer, ATS-friendly and tailored" is three promises. The pitch has not been reduced to one sentence, and the brief's audience is still an ASSUMPTION covering new grads, people 0-5 years into work and career switchers. These are different products. The 7-day revenue goal pushes toward taking any order, which works against focus.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- What is the one thing a customer gets here that they can't get from a free AI tool in ten minutes?
- Walk me through the customer's first five minutes after paying. What do you ask them, and how?
- Which one group of buyers do you serve first, and who do you turn away?
- Show me a before/after you made. Would you put your name on it?
- At $35 and a 48-hour turnaround, how much time can you spend on each resume without cutting corners? (CFO question: /founder-cfo)
- Will buyers pay for human judgment on top of AI, and at what price? (/founder-consumer; for what others charge, /founder-competitors)

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. A one-sentence promise with one buyer segment chosen. Everything else is cut from the pitch.
2. A written intake form and delivery checklist covering every step from first message to revision, so there are no ad-hoc handoffs.
3. Three real before/after samples, done to a standard the founder would show publicly, before any paid order is taken.
4. A clear statement of what the founder adds beyond the AI draft (for example, interview questions that draw out real achievements), shown in the samples.
