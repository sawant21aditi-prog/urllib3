# Board summary: Resume Rewrite Service

## The vote
**2 fund if, 1 pass.** Average score **3.3 / 10**.

| Lens | Score | Vote |
|---|---|---|
| Offers | 4 | FUND IF |
| Monopoly | 2 | PASS |
| Product | 4 | FUND IF |

## Risks raised by more than one member (most dangerous first)
1. **Free AI does the same thing** (all three). Any buyer can paste their resume into a free AI tool. As pitched, the service is a lightly edited AI draft, which makes it a commodity.
2. **No proof and no confirmed expertise** (all three). The seller has no reviews, no before/after samples and no confirmed resume or hiring experience, so a stranger has no reason to believe it works.
3. **The audience is too broad** (all three). New grads, early-career workers and career switchers are different buyers who need different products. There is no small niche to own first.
4. **The $1,000-in-7-days goal works against the business** (Offers, Monopoly, Product). At $35 it needs about 29 paid orders in a week from a seller with zero reviews. The deadline pushes toward taking any order and competing on price.

## Where they disagree
- **Monopoly passes outright.** No moat applies, and a copycat can start in month one.
- **Offers and Product fund it with conditions.** The pain is real, buyers are easy to reach for free, delivery is cheap, and $50 is a cheap lesson. They think a niche plus proof can fix it.

## Conditions checklist (the rest of the pack tests these)
- [ ] Pick **one narrow buyer and one outcome**, ideally tied to a field the founder knows from the inside. (Offers, Product; would reopen Monopoly)
- [ ] Write a **one-sentence promise** for that buyer and cut everything else from the pitch. (Product)
- [ ] Name **what the founder adds that free AI does not**: an intake interview that draws out real achievements, tailored versions for specific job posts, a LinkedIn headline/summary, a written explanation of the changes. (Offers, Product)
- [ ] Make **3–5 before/after samples** (free or discounted) in exchange for testimonials, before taking paid orders. (Offers, Product)
- [ ] Write an **intake form and delivery checklist** covering every step from first message to revision. (Product)
- [ ] Add a **real guarantee** the numbers can afford. (Offers; test with `/founder-cfo`)
- [ ] Confirm **what others charge** and whether any seller already owns the niche. (`/founder-competitors`)
- [ ] Confirm the niche's buyers **will pay a specialist over free AI**, and at what price. (`/founder-consumer`, `/founder-pricing`)
- [ ] Confirm **hours per order and the margin** at the chosen price. (`/founder-cfo`)
- [ ] Treat **$1,000 in 7 days as a test target**. Week 1 succeeds if strangers pay at all.

## The strongest version the board can see
This is **not** what was pitched. A generic $35 "AI-assisted resume rewrite for anyone" lost to free AI tools in all three memos. The strongest version is a **specialist**: resumes for one kind of job seeker the founder understands from the inside (one industry, one role, or one type of career switch). It is sold as a bundle free AI can't give: a short intake interview that draws out real achievements, the resume, tailored versions for the buyer's actual target job posts, and a LinkedIn summary. It launches with a handful of free samples to earn proof, then charges more than $35, because specialists aren't compared on price. Revenue in week 1 will probably be small. Proof is what that week should produce.
